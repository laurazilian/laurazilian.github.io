Research

Two lines of work, one toolkit. I look at how labour markets and family life change, and at who gains and who loses from that change.

Work, skills and digital change

Does digitalisation polarise the labour market, or does it raise skill requirements across the board? Using Austrian online vacancy data, my co-authors and I found no evidence of polarisation, but a broad trend towards upskilling. Building on this, we developed an indicator of how digital an occupation is, based on the skills employers ask for. We also mapped how workplace skills are distributed across occupations as a network.

On the worker side, I have studied digital inequality using the OECD PIAAC survey, the gender dimension of digitalisation, and how mismatches between digital skills and job requirements shape unemployment. The latter uses agent-based simulation of the Austrian labour market.

Partnership, family and gender

At the University of Vienna I work with Austrian register data on couples. I study how local mating markets and education-specific “mating squeezes” shape partnership formation, and why hypogamous unions (where the woman has more education) are on the rise. I also look at the social gradient in infant health from a couple-level perspective, and at how a first birth affects poverty risk within couples.

In the new project STEMPATHS we ask how families shape women's choice of a STEM field and whether they stay in it.
